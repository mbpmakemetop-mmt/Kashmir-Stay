10 Must-Try Kashmiri Foods (And Where to Eat Them in Srinagar)

Kashmiri cuisine is built around rich, slow-cooked meat curries, fragrant rice, and a tea culture unlike anywhere else in India. Here's what to actually order, and how to find it.

Kashmiri food rarely gets the spotlight in India's broader food conversation, which is a shame — it has its own distinct spice profile (led by dried Kashmiri red chili, fennel, and dry ginger rather than the turmeric-heavy base common elsewhere), and a strong tradition of hospitality built around feeding guests generously. Here are the ten dishes worth seeking out.

1Rogan Josh

Kashmir's most famous export — tender mutton slow-cooked in a deep red gravy, colored by Kashmiri red chili (which brings color more than intense heat) and flavored with fennel and ginger powder. Available at almost every Kashmiri restaurant and a staple of the Wazwan feast.

2Yakhni

A yogurt-based mutton curry, milder and lighter than Rogan Josh, flavored with fennel and cardamom rather than chili. A good pick if you want the richness of Kashmiri meat cooking without the heat or deep color of Rogan Josh.

3Gushtaba

Minced mutton pounded into smooth meatballs and cooked in a yogurt gravy. Traditionally the final dish served at a Wazwan feast, considered something of a closing showpiece.

4Rista

Similar preparation to Gushtaba but cooked in a red gravy instead of yogurt — minced mutton balls in a Rogan Josh-style sauce. Often served alongside Gushtaba for contrast.

5Dum Aloo (Kashmiri style)

Small whole potatoes, deep-fried then simmered in a yogurt-based spiced gravy. A vegetarian favorite and one of the easiest Kashmiri dishes to find outside dedicated Wazwan restaurants.

6Nadru Yakhni / Nadru Monji

Lotus stem (nadru) is a genuine Kashmiri specialty, cooked either in a yogurt gravy (Yakhni style) or battered and fried (Monji). A great vegetarian option and something you won't find easily outside the valley.

7Modur Pulao

A mildly sweet rice dish cooked with milk, dry fruits, saffron, and a touch of sugar — served as part of festive meals, a nice contrast to the richer meat dishes around it.

8Kashmiri Kahwa

Not a dish but essential to the experience — a green tea infused with saffron, cinnamon, cardamom, and almonds, traditionally served in a copper samovar. Widely available at cafes, restaurants, and offered as hospitality in most homes and houseboats.

9Harissa

A slow-cooked, pounded meat porridge traditionally eaten in winter mornings, rich and warming. It's more of a seasonal, local breakfast item than a tourist-restaurant staple, so you may need to ask locally or visit early morning eateries in the old city to find it.

10Kashmiri Girda / Tsot (Local Breads)

Kashmir has its own bread culture, sold fresh every morning from local bakeries (kandur wan). Girda is a soft, round leavened bread, while Tsot is a crisper, denser variety — both are typically eaten with butter and Kahwa or noon chai (pink salted tea) for breakfast.

Where to actually eat this: Established Wazwan restaurants in Srinagar (particularly around Residency Road and the old city) serve most of these dishes reliably. But for the most authentic version, ask your houseboat host if they serve home-cooked Kashmiri meals — many houseboats include breakfast and dinner, and home cooking is often closer to how these dishes are meant to taste than a restaurant version.

A Note on Wazwan

If you get invited to or book a Wazwan experience, know that it's traditionally a multi-course feast (up to 36 dishes at full weddings, though tourist versions are usually a curated 7-11 course selection) eaten communally from a large shared plate called a trami, typically for four people. It's a serious culinary tradition prepared by trained chefs called wazas, passed down through families for generations.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the most famous Kashmiri dish?

Rogan Josh, a slow-cooked mutton curry in a deep red gravy, is generally considered Kashmir's most internationally recognized dish, though Wazwan as a whole (the traditional multi-course feast) is the broader culinary highlight of the region.

Is Kashmiri food very spicy?

Kashmiri food uses generous amounts of spices for flavor and color (like Kashmiri red chili, which is milder and more about color than heat), but most dishes are not extremely spicy-hot. There are milder options widely available for those sensitive to spice.

What is Wazwan?

Wazwan is a traditional multi-course Kashmiri feast, often served at weddings and special occasions, that can include up to 36 dishes prepared mostly from meat, cooked by a team of chefs called wazas.

Where can I try authentic Kashmiri food in Srinagar?

Several established Srinagar restaurants specialize in Wazwan and Kashmiri cuisine, and many houseboats also serve home-cooked Kashmiri meals as part of the stay — ask your houseboat host, as home-cooked versions are often more authentic than restaurant versions.
