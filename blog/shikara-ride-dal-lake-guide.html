Shikara Ride on Dal Lake: Prices, Timings & Tips

A shikara ride is the one Kashmir experience almost every visitor has on their list — here's what it actually costs, how long to budget, and how to make the most of it.

Quick Facts

Typical price (1 hour)₹400 - ₹800
Typical duration30 min - 2 hours (1 hour most common)
Best timeSunrise or sunset
Where to boardMain ghats — Nehru Park, Boulevard Road, Ghat No. 1-8
CapacityTypically 2-4 people per shikara

What to Expect

A shikara is a traditional wooden boat, gently paddled by a boatman, used both for tourist rides and by locals to move around Dal Lake day-to-day. A standard tourist ride covers the floating vegetable gardens, Nehru Park, the houseboat rows along the lake edge, and often passes by local shikaras selling flowers, jewelry, saffron, or snacks — a normal and generally low-pressure part of the experience, though you're free to politely decline.

Pricing — What You'll Actually Pay

Rates are technically fixed by an official tariff board maintained by the Tourism Department, priced per hour rather than per person, but real-world pricing varies with season, negotiation, and which ghat you board from. As a rough guide:

  • Short ride (30 min): ₹250-450
  • Standard ride (1 hour): ₹400-800
  • Extended ride (2 hours, further into the lake/Nigeen Lake side): ₹800-1,500

Prices tend to run higher near the most convenient tourist ghats and during peak season (May-June, Dec-Jan). If you're staying on a houseboat, your host can often arrange a shikara at a fair local rate, sometimes as part of your stay package for airport-to-houseboat transfers.

Best Time of Day

Early morning (around sunrise) gives you calm water, soft light, and far fewer boats — a noticeably different, quieter experience than midday. Sunset is the more popular choice for obvious reasons (golden light on the water, mountain silhouettes) but also the busiest time, so expect more boat traffic and a livelier atmosphere rather than a quiet one.

Tip: Agree on the exact price AND duration before boarding, ideally in writing or at least clearly repeated back to the boatman, to avoid any confusion or upselling once you're out on the water.

What to Bring

  • A light jacket or shawl — it gets noticeably cooler on the water, even in summer
  • Cash in small denominations for the ride and any purchases from vendor shikaras
  • A camera or phone with a strap — dropping something in Dal Lake is a real (if uncommon) risk

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a shikara ride cost on Dal Lake?

A standard shikara ride on Dal Lake typically costs ₹400-800 for a 1-hour ride, though official rates are set per hour and can vary by season and negotiation. Longer rides or sunset-specific timing may cost more.

How long does a shikara ride last?

Most tourist shikara rides last around 1 hour, which is enough to cover key sights like the floating gardens, Nehru Park, and houseboat rows. Shorter 30-minute rides and longer 2-hour rides are also commonly available.

What is the best time for a shikara ride?

Sunrise and sunset are widely considered the best times for a shikara ride on Dal Lake, offering the calmest water and best light. Early morning also tends to have fewer boats on the lake.

Is it necessary to negotiate the shikara price?

Official rate boards exist at main ghats, but many tourists still negotiate, particularly for longer rides or off-season visits. Agreeing on the price and duration clearly before boarding avoids misunderstandings.
